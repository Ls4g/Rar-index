# Idea: magnetic display case for manga

Parked 12 August 2026. Not on the roadmap — a note so it is not lost.

## The idea

A case a manga volume sits inside, which then holds magnetically to a wall
board, so books can be displayed face-out rather than spine-out.

## Why it came up

The r/MangaCollectors research (see the audience briefings) found shelving
and storage to be the single most-discussed topic — 311 mentions across the
comment trees of the eighteen most-discussed threads, well ahead of price at
202. 95% of posts in that community are photographs of collections. Display
is not a side interest there; it is the main activity.

Spine-out shelving hides the cover art, which is the thing collectors
actually photograph and care about. Face-out display answers that directly.

## The three things that decide it

**1. Manga is not one size.** A Japanese Jump Comics volume (shinsho-ban) is
narrower and shorter than a VIZ English tankōbon, which differs again from a
deluxe edition or an omnibus. One case cannot fit all of them. At least
three SKUs would be needed on day one, and getting the fit wrong means
returns and bad reviews.

**2. The board is the real cost, not the case.** A magnetic wall panel large
enough for even twenty volumes is expensive and needs mounting, and much of
this audience rents. IKEA's MOSSLANDA picture ledge is around £5, displays
books face-out already, and is landlord-friendly. That is the competitor,
not other cases.

**3. The economics only work for a selection.** At roughly £10 a case,
someone with 300 volumes is being pitched £3,000. This is a "display my
fifteen favourites" product rather than a shelving system. That is a viable
product, but it is a different pitch and a much smaller basket.

## Where it connects to RAR

RAR already stores `format` (e.g. 新書判 / shinsho-ban), publisher and page
count per edition. That makes RAR the only thing that could tell a collector
**which case fits which book they already own**, read straight off their
portfolio. That is a real link rather than a shop bolted onto a catalogue,
and it would be the reason to build the two together rather than separately.

## Honest caveat

This is a different business from the software: manufacturing, tooling,
inventory, shipping, returns. The demand signal is real and well evidenced;
the operational load is not comparable to shipping a feature.
